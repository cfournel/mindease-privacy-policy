Self-hypnosis for anxiety

A guided session that slows the body first, then works on the anxious thought itself — written around what you are actually worried about.

Why hypnosis helps with anxiety

Anxiety keeps two systems busy at once: a body braced for something, and a mind rehearsing it. Guided hypnosis addresses the first directly — slow, paced language and steady breathing shift you out of the alert state — which is what makes the second easier to loosen.

In that settled state, suggestion lands differently. Instead of arguing with an anxious thought, a session offers a calmer version of the same scene and lets you rehearse it, so the next time it comes up it carries less charge.

What a session works on

  • Physical tension — jaw, shoulders, chest, stomach — released stage by stage.
  • The loop of anticipating what might go wrong, replaced with a rehearsed calm response.
  • An anchor you can use later: a breath, a word, or a gesture that recalls the session's state.
  • A specific worry, if you type one in — an interview, a flight, a conversation you are dreading.

How Onira builds your session

  1. You pick a theme and, if you want, type a sentence or two about what is actually going on.
  2. An AI model running on your phone writes the script — induction, deepening, imagery, suggestions, anchoring, then a clear return to full alertness.
  3. Narration starts as soon as the opening is ready and the rest keeps writing while you listen, so a full 15 to 30 minute session begins in seconds.
  4. Keep a session you liked and replay the exact same narration whenever you want.

What to expect

Sessions run roughly 15 to 30 minutes. You stay aware throughout — hypnosis is focused attention, not sleep or unconsciousness — and the session always closes with a deliberate return to full alertness. Most people use headphones, sitting or lying somewhere they will not be interrupted.

Common questions

Does self-hypnosis actually work for anxiety?

Guided relaxation and suggestion are widely used as a self-help tool for everyday anxiety and stress, and many people find them calming. Onira is a relaxation tool, not a treatment for an anxiety disorder — that is something to discuss with a clinician.

Can I do this at bedtime?

Yes, though the session ends by bringing you back to alertness. If your goal is to fall asleep, the sleep theme is written for that instead.

Do I have to type anything personal?

No. The theme alone is enough. If you do type something, it stays on your phone — the model runs locally and nothing is uploaded.

Private by design
