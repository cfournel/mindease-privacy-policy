Self-hypnosis for focus

A shorter, brighter session to clear the noise before study, writing, or work that needs sustained attention.

Why hypnosis helps with focus

Losing focus is usually not a shortage of attention but a surplus of competing claims on it — notifications, half-finished tasks, the low hum of everything else you should be doing. Attention is already trained on something; it is just not the thing in front of you.

Hypnosis is, at its simplest, sustained focused attention. A session narrows the field deliberately, sets aside what is not for now, and rehearses starting — which is usually the hardest part of a long stretch of work.

What a session works on

  • Narrowing attention to one task and setting the rest down for a defined period.
  • The friction of starting, rehearsed until the first minute is unremarkable.
  • Returning to the task after an interruption without losing the thread.
  • A specific piece of work, if you name it — an exam, a chapter, a build, a deadline.

How Onira builds your session

  1. You pick a theme and, if you want, type a sentence or two about what is actually going on.
  2. An AI model running on your phone writes the script — induction, deepening, imagery, suggestions, anchoring, then a clear return to full alertness.
  3. Narration starts as soon as the opening is ready and the rest keeps writing while you listen, so a full 15 to 30 minute session begins in seconds.
  4. Keep a session you liked and replay the exact same narration whenever you want.

What to expect

Use a focus session immediately before the work itself. It ends clearly alert and forward-leaning rather than sleepy, so you can move straight into the task.

Common questions

Should I listen while working?

No — listen first, then work. The session asks for your attention; the task needs it afterwards.

Is this useful for studying?

That is one of the most common uses. Naming the subject and the exam date makes the session noticeably more specific.

Can I make it shorter?

Sessions target roughly 15 to 30 minutes. You can stop at any point — nothing breaks if you end early.
